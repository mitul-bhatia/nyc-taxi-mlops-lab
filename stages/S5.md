# S5: observability (Day 2, 10:45–12:00)

**Goal:** you can see what riders get (errors, latency, and whether the model or the fallback answered), you've said what "good enough" means as SLOs, and an alert tells you when you're failing them, all inside Grafana Cloud's free tier.

## Recall check (5 minutes, no agent)

1. SLI, SLO, SLA: one sentence each, with an example from this app.
2. Why does a latency SLO use a histogram and a percentile, not an average?
3. What makes a Prometheus series new? Name two labels that would blow up a series count.
4. What is a burn rate, and why page on a fast burn over two windows rather than on any error?

## Given

- `contracts/metrics.yaml` (frozen): what the app and serving expose on `/metrics`, with their labels and buckets. Traefik exposes per-service request metrics too. None of it is reachable from the internet: Traefik doesn't route `/metrics`.
- Alloy, on the app and platform hosts: `deploy/compose.observability.yaml`, `observability/alloy/` (`app.alloy`, `platform.alloy`, `common.alloy`), deployed by `.github/workflows/observability.yaml`. It scrapes the host, every container (cAdvisor), and the lab's services, and sends everything through `budget.alloy`, which has no rules yet.
- `observability/push-rules.sh`: promtool checks and tests your rules, then loads them into Grafana Cloud. `make check` runs the promtool part.
- `observability/dashboards/ride-app.json`: a dashboard with riders, models and hosts done, and the SLO row left for you.
- `observability/alloy-stats.sh`: what Alloy is sending. The status workflow now includes it, so the grader can see your series count.

## Build

1. **Connect (15 min):** in Grafana Cloud, create an access policy token with `metrics:write`, `metrics:read`, `rules:write` and `rules:read`. Note your Prometheus remote-write URL and user (Home → your stack → Prometheus → Details). The remote-write URL ends in `/api/prom/push`; the same page shows a query URL without `/push`, which is not it. Add a `lab/grafana` secret container to `infra/live/main.tf` with the given `secret` module and apply. Then put the value from your laptop, so it never touches Terraform state or GitHub:
   `aws secretsmanager put-secret-value --secret-id lab/grafana --secret-string '{"url": "https://…/api/prom/push", "user": "<user>", "token": "<token>"}'`.
   Then `gh variable set LAB_OBSERVABILITY --body on` and `gh workflow run observability.yaml`.
2. **Count (10 min):** with no rules, everything goes out. In Grafana's Explore, run `count by (host, job) ({__name__=~".+"})`. How far are you from 10,000 series, and what will the LLM host add in S6?
3. **Budget (15 min):** write `budget.alloy`'s rules: keep what a dashboard, an SLO or an incident would use, and drop the rest. Watch for labels that change on every deploy. Push, then deploy the app three times in a row (`gh workflow run cd.yaml`) and count again.
4. **SLOs (20 min):** write `observability/slo.yaml` (what you measure, the objective, why) and `observability/rules/slo.rules.yaml` (recording rules for each SLI and burn-rate alerts). Add a `*.test.yaml` with promtool unit tests: an outage pages, a short spike doesn't. `make check` runs them, and the observability workflow loads them.
5. **See it fire (10 min):** import the dashboard, add a panel per SLO, then break something a rider would notice. For example, stop `serving-champion` over SSM (`docker stop lab-serving-serving-champion-1`) while sending requests. Does your alert fire, and how long did it take? Start it again.

## Decision points (one record each in `decisions/`)

- **SLO targets:** which SLIs, what objective for each, and why that number and not a stricter one.
- **Series budget:** what you keep, what you drop, and how far under 10,000 you need to be for the rest of the day.

## Done when

- The observability workflow is green: Alloy on both hosts sending, none refused, and your rules loaded.
- The status workflow shows your hosts' active series under budget after a burst of deploys.
- `make check` passes with your rules and their tests.
- One of your alerts has fired for a real reason, and you have the timeline (break, alert, fix).
- The dashboard JSON with your SLO panels is committed, and your two new decision records exist.
